How Overtime Is Calculated in California

California is one of the few US states that pays overtime on a daily basis as well as a weekly one. That means a long Tuesday can trigger overtime even when the week totals 35 hours — and the two tests do not stack on top of each other.

Last updated 18 November 2026

The two tests, and why they do not add up

Most US states follow the federal rule: overtime starts after 40 hours in a workweek. California adds a second, independent test based on the individual day. Both run at the same time, and you are paid under whichever produces the larger amount for that workweek — never both added together.

SituationRate
More than 8 hours in a single workday1.5× for those hours
More than 12 hours in a single workday2× for hours beyond 12
More than 40 hours in a workweek1.5× for those hours
7th consecutive day worked in a workweek1.5× for the first 8 hours, 2× after that

The 7th-day rule is the one most people have never heard of. If you work seven days in a row within the same workweek, the seventh day is paid at 1.5× regardless of how many hours you worked, and anything beyond 8 hours on that day is paid at 2×.

A worked example

You earn $20.00 an hour and work 13 hours on a single day.

8 hours × $20.00 = $160.00 (straight time) 4 hours × $30.00 = $120.00 (1.5× for hours 9–12) 1 hour × $40.00 = $40.00 (2× for hour 13) total = $320.00

That is 16 hours of pay for 13 hours of work. If the same 13 hours had been worked across two normal days — say 8 and 5 — the daily test would never fire, and you would be paid straight time for all of it unless the week crossed 40 hours.

This is why scheduling matters as much as total hours in California. Two 10-hour days and one 8-hour day is 28 hours with 4 hours of daily overtime. Redistributing the same 28 hours as four 7-hour days produces no overtime at all.

The regular rate is not your base rate

California requires overtime to be calculated on the regular rate of pay, which is generally higher than the base hourly rate. It includes:

  • Shift differentials for evenings, nights, or weekends
  • Non-discretionary bonuses — production, attendance, commission tied to targets
  • Certain incentive payments and longevity pay

If you earn $20.00 an hour plus a $2.00 night differential, your regular rate for overtime purposes is $22.00, and the overtime rate is $33.00 rather than $30.00. Employers that apply 1.5× to the base rate alone underpay, and the shortfall compounds over a year.

New York has daily overtime only for certain occupations, which is why "does New York have daily overtime" produces conflicting answers online. For most private-sector workers there, the weekly test is the only one.

Where people get it wrong

  • Adding the two tests. A 50-hour week made of five 10-hour days is 10 hours of overtime, not 10 daily plus 10 weekly.
  • Ignoring the 7th day. A six-day schedule is common in hospitality and construction; the seventh day converts the whole shift to premium pay.
  • Using the base rate. Overtime on $20.00 when the regular rate is $22.00 understates every premium hour.
  • Assuming a salary removes overtime. California's exemption tests are strict, and a salaried employee whose duties are primarily manual or routine is generally non-exempt.
  • Not recording hours. The burden of proving hours worked falls largely on the employer in California, but a personal record is what makes a claim practical.

If you think you were underpaid

Start with your own timesheet: dates, start and end times including any work after clocking out, and the pay you actually received. Compare that against the rules above for each workweek separately — the workweek is fixed and seven days long, and it does not shift to avoid overtime.

California's statute of limitations is generally three years for overtime claims under the Labor Code, longer than most states. The state Labor Commissioner handles wage claims without a filing fee, and a written record is the difference between a claim and a complaint.

Frequently asked questions

Is overtime after 8 hours or 40 hours in California?

Both. More than 8 hours in a workday is paid at 1.5×, and more than 40 hours in a workweek is also paid at 1.5×. The two tests run independently and you receive the higher amount, not the sum.

When does double time apply in California?

Hours beyond 12 in a single workday, and all hours beyond 8 on the seventh consecutive day worked in a workweek. Double time means 2× the regular rate, not 1.5×.

Does the 7th consecutive day rule apply to any seven days?

No. The seven days must fall within the same workweek. A Friday-to-Thursday run that spans two workweeks does not trigger it, because the count resets when the workweek does.

Are salaried employees in California entitled to overtime?

Only if they are exempt, which requires a salary above the threshold, payment on a salary basis, and duties that are primarily executive, administrative, or professional. A salaried employee doing mostly manual work is generally non-exempt and owed overtime.

Is a shift differential included in the overtime rate?

Yes. Shift differentials are part of the regular rate of pay, so overtime is calculated on base pay plus the differential, not on base pay alone.

What if my employer rounds my clock-in times?

Rounding is permitted only if it does not systematically underpay over time. Rounding that consistently shaves minutes in the employer's favour is unlawful, and the pattern is usually visible over a few weeks of records.
