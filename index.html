Why these six

Most financial calculators assume a stable salaried job with a payslip that arrives on the same date every month. The numbers people actually get stuck on are messier: a shift that ran three hours long, a wage that has to be compared against a salary offer, a tip pool that four people need to agree on before anyone goes home.

Each tool here answers one question properly rather than twenty questions approximately. Where a rule depends on jurisdiction — overtime thresholds, tip-pool law, tax rates — the page explains the actual rule and the calculator lets you set the parameter yourself, instead of hiding a single assumption.

How the numbers are produced

Every calculation runs in your browser in plain JavaScript. Nothing you type is transmitted, stored on a server, or logged. The formulas are printed on each page next to a worked example so you can check the arithmetic by hand if the result matters to a decision.

That matters more than it sounds. A calculator with a hidden assumption is just an opinion with a decimal point. Where a model is a simplification — the sleep debt recovery schedule is the clearest example — the page says so, because a confident wrong number is worse than an honest range.

What these calculators are not

  • Not tax advice. Figures are gross unless stated. Actual take-home depends on your jurisdiction, filing status, and the deductions on your payslip.
  • Not legal advice. Overtime and tip-pool rules vary by state and country and change. The pages summarise the rules to make the calculator usable; they do not replace your contract or a labour authority.
  • Not financial advice. Projections use constant assumptions. Real returns and real inflation are not constant, and a fixed average return cannot show a bad year arriving at the worst moment.
  • Not medical advice. Sleep debt is a planning heuristic. Persistent sleep problems need a clinician, not a calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is Workmath really free?

Yes. The calculators are free and require no account. The site is supported by advertising, which is why you may see ads on the pages; the calculators themselves do not collect the numbers you enter.

Do you store what I type?

No. All arithmetic happens locally in your browser. Only your currency preference is saved, in your own browser's local storage, and nothing else leaves the page.

Which currency should I choose?

The selector in the header works across all six calculators and remembers your choice. It changes the symbol and number formatting only — the maths is currency-neutral, so figures are equally valid in dollars, pounds, euros or rupees.

Can I use these on my phone?

Yes. The layout is responsive and the inputs use numeric keypads on mobile. The pages also work offline once loaded, and print cleanly if you need a record of a calculation.

How accurate are the results?

The arithmetic is exact to the cent for the inputs you provide; the uncertainty is in the assumptions. Hourly-to-salary depends on the hours you really work, savings projections depend on the return and inflation you enter, and sleep debt depends on self-reported hours. Set realistic inputs and the output is as good as the input.
