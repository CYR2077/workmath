Is $30 an Hour Good Money?

$30 an hour is $62,400 a year before tax. Whether that is good depends on three things the rate itself cannot tell you: where you live, how many people it supports, and what your fixed costs already are.

Last updated 18 November 2026

The number, and where it sits

$30.00 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $62,400

Median full-time hourly earnings in the United States sit near $30, which makes this rate roughly the middle of the distribution — half of full-time hourly workers earn more, half earn less. It is clearly above the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and above most state minimums.

Compared with salaried work, $62,400 is a respectable single income. Compared with the cost of housing in a major coastal city, it is a budget that requires decisions. Both statements are true at once, which is why national averages are a poor guide to a personal decision.

The 30% housing test

A long-standing rule of thumb: rent should not exceed 30% of gross income. At $62,400 that is $1,560 a month.

In a city where a one-bedroom runs $2,400, $30 an hour is not a housing-secure income for a single earner. In a mid-size market where the same apartment is $1,100, it is comfortable with room to save. The rate did not change; the city did.

One income or two people

The same $62,400 supports very different lives depending on who it covers.

  • Single adult, low fixed costs: workable almost anywhere, with room for savings and a modest emergency fund.
  • Single adult, car payment and student loans: workable in most of the country, thin in expensive metros.
  • Couple with one earner: tight in mid-cost cities, difficult in high-cost ones.
  • Family with children: generally not enough on one income once childcare is counted — full-time childcare commonly runs $800–$2,000 a month per child.

Childcare is the cost that changes the answer most abruptly, because it is large, non-negotiable, and arrives suddenly. A budget that works at $30 an hour without children can fail at the same rate with one.

What the rate leaves out

Four costs that never appear in an hourly-to-salary comparison but change the disposable income substantially:

  1. Health insurance. If the employer does not cover it, a single adult plan can run $300–$700 a month. That is $2–$4 an hour of the rate.
  2. Commute. A job that requires a car adds fuel, insurance, maintenance, and depreciation — commonly $400–$800 a month in the US.
  3. Unpaid time off. If the role has no paid leave, two weeks off is $2,400 of income that does not arrive.
  4. Schedule volatility. A nominal $30 with hours that swing between 25 and 45 is not the same as a guaranteed 40. Annualise the realistic average, not the best week.

Two jobs at the same $30 an hour can differ by $8,000–$10,000 a year in real terms once these are counted. The rate is the least informative part of the offer.

How to judge it for yourself

Ignore the national median and run three personal numbers instead:

  1. Annual gross: realistic hours × rate × 52. Not the best week, the average week.
  2. Net after deductions: subtract 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, then estimate federal and state income tax, then subtract any benefits you pay for.
  3. Fixed costs: rent or mortgage, utilities, transport, insurance, debt minimums, childcare. Subtract from net.

What remains is the only figure that answers the question. If it is positive and covers a 10% savings rate, $30 is a good rate for your situation. If it is negative, the rate is not the problem to solve first — the fixed costs or the household structure are.

Where $30 an hour goes next

If the role is non-exempt, overtime is the fastest lever: five extra hours a week at 1.5× adds about $11,700 a year, which is worth more than a $3 an hour raise at 40 hours.

If it is exempt, the lever is the hours themselves. A salaried role paying the equivalent of $30 at 40 hours becomes $26 an hour at 46 hours, and that erosion is usually invisible because the paycheque does not change.

Frequently asked questions

Is $30 an hour a good wage?

It is around the median for full-time hourly workers in the US and clearly above minimum wage. Whether it is good depends on housing costs where you live and how many people it supports — $62,400 is comfortable for a single earner in most of the country and tight in major coastal cities.

How much is $30 an hour per month?

$5,200 gross before tax, which is $62,400 divided by 12. After Social Security and Medicare at 7.65% that is about $4,802, before income tax and any benefit deductions.

Is $30 an hour enough to live on?

For a single adult outside the most expensive metros, generally yes. For a family on one income, usually not once childcare and health insurance are counted. The rate is only half the calculation; household size and fixed costs are the other half.

What is $30 an hour annually with overtime?

Five hours of overtime a week at 1.5× adds $225 weekly, taking the annual figure from $62,400 to about $74,100. Ten hours a week takes it to roughly $85,800, assuming the overtime is paid as federal law requires.

How does $30 an hour compare to a $60,000 salary?

They are close on paper — $62,400 against $60,000 — but the hourly role usually pays overtime and the salaried one usually does not. At 45 hours a week the salary is worth about $26.67 an hour; the hourly rate with five hours of overtime is worth about $31.73.
