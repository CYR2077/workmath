How overtime pay is calculated

US federal overtime comes from the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). It sets a floor, not a ceiling: 1.5 times the employee's regular rate of pay for every hour worked beyond 40 in a single workweek. An employer may pay more, and several states do require more, but nobody covered by the Act may pay less.

weekly gross = (regular hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × multiplier)
overtime premium = overtime hours × rate × (multiplier − 1)

The distinction between overtime pay and overtime premium matters more than it looks. Your regular rate already covers every hour you worked, so the extra cost of overtime is only the half-time portion. When you are comparing two job offers, the premium is the number that tells you what those extra hours are actually worth to you.

A worked example

You earn $20.00 an hour and work 45 hours in a week. The first 40 hours are straight time: 40 × $20.00 = $800.00. The remaining 5 hours are paid at 1.5×: 5 × $30.00 = $150.00. Total gross is $950.00, and your effective hourly rate across all 45 hours is $950.00 ÷ 45 = $21.11.

Notice that the five overtime hours raised your average rate by only $1.11, not by $10.00. This is the single most common place people go wrong when they mentally "add time and a half" to a weekly figure.

Your regular rate is not always your hourly wage

The FLSA regular rate includes more than the base rate. It generally covers:

  • Shift differentials for evenings, nights, or weekends
  • Non-discretionary bonuses — production bonuses, attendance bonuses, commissions tied to a target
  • Longevity pay and certain incentive payments
  • Value of meals or lodging provided for the employer's convenience

It generally excludes discretionary bonuses, expense reimbursements, gifts, holiday pay when no work is performed, and employer contributions to benefit plans. If your pay includes a shift differential or a recurring bonus, add it into the base rate field before calculating, or the overtime will be understated.

Weekly versus daily overtime

Federal law recognises only the workweek test. Some states add a daily test, which means you can be owed overtime for a long Tuesday even if the week totals 38 hours. Where both tests exist, you are paid under whichever produces the higher total for that week — not both, stacked.

Who is exempt and who is not

Exemption is decided by duties and pay structure, not by a job title. Under the FLSA a salaried employee is generally exempt only when all three of these hold: they are paid a predetermined salary that does not change with quality or quantity of work, that salary meets the applicable threshold, and their primary duties are executive, administrative, professional, outside sales, or certain computer roles.

A "salaried" warehouse supervisor who spends most of the shift on the floor loading trucks is usually non-exempt and owed overtime, whatever the offer letter says. If you suspect misclassification, the practical evidence is your own timesheet — hours worked, dates, and the pay you actually received.

Common overtime mistakes

  • Averaging across two weeks. The workweek is fixed and seven days long. Forty-five hours one week and 35 the next is 5 hours of overtime, not zero.
  • Ignoring "off the clock" work. Answering messages, finishing a shift handover, or clocking out then continuing is compensable time if the employer knew or should have known about it.
  • Using the wrong base rate. Applying 1.5× to base pay while ignoring a shift differential understates the regular rate.
  • Treating a fixed "overtime allowance" as satisfying the obligation. A flat stipend does not replace the legal premium unless it is genuinely computed on hours over the threshold.
  • Forgetting the extra half on double-time states. In California, hours over 12 in a day are paid at 2× the regular rate — the calculator handles this once you set the double-time field.

Frequently asked questions

Is overtime always 1.5 times my hourly rate?

Under federal law the minimum is 1.5× your regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. States can set higher requirements, and an employer can voluntarily pay more. Your regular rate is not always your base wage, because non-discretionary bonuses and shift differentials are included in it.

Does overtime start after 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week?

Federally it is weekly only. Daily overtime after 8 hours is a state rule — California, Alaska, Nevada for lower-paid workers, and Colorado above 12 hours. In those states both tests run and you receive the higher amount.

How do I calculate my effective hourly rate with overtime?

Divide total weekly pay by total hours worked. At $20 an hour and 45 hours, that is $950 ÷ 45 = $21.11. It is the honest number to compare against a salaried offer that expects the same hours.

Are salaried employees entitled to overtime?

Only if they are non-exempt. Exemption depends on the salary basis, the salary level, and the duties test — not the job title. Misclassifying an employee does not remove the obligation.

Can my employer give comp time instead of overtime pay?

For private employers in the US, generally no. Comp time in lieu of overtime cash is limited to public sector employers under specific conditions. Adjusting a schedule inside the same workweek so hours stay at or below 40 is a different practice and is allowed.

How is overtime calculated for two jobs?

Separately per employer, per workweek. Hours from two different employers are not combined. If one employer pays you two rates in the same week, use the weighted average as the regular rate.
