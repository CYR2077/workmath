How a tip pool is usually divided

A pooled house collects tips into one pot and redistributes them by a written formula. The two methods that survive contact with a real rota are hours worked and weighted points.

By hours worked

share_i = pool × (hours_i ÷ total hours)

Simple, transparent, and hard to argue with. If the pool is $840 and the team worked 70 hours combined, each hour is worth $12.00. Someone who worked a 7-hour dinner shift takes $84.00; someone who did a 12-hour double takes $144.00.

The weakness is that hours are not contribution. Two servers can work the same five hours while one turns eight tables and the other turns three. Most houses accept that, because the alternative is a judgement call that someone has to make every night.

By points

points_i = hours_i × role weight
share_i = pool × (points_i ÷ total points)

Points fix the role problem: a bartender on 1.25 points per hour, a server on 1.0, a busser on 0.75. A bartender working 6 hours earns 7.5 points; a server working 8 hours earns 8.0. The bartender still takes home less, but less by a much smaller margin than raw hours would produce.

Whatever weights you choose, they belong in writing and they belong in the same document every shift. Retroactive weighting is the fastest way to lose a team.

Why the cents never add up on paper

If three people split $100 three ways the exact shares are $33.333… each. Round each to $33.33 and you are a cent short. Pay two at $33.33 and one at $33.34 and everybody can see who got the extra cent. The practical rule: calculate exact shares, round down, then hand the leftover cents to the largest shares first. The calculator's whole-unit rounding option does exactly this so the total always lands on the pool amount.

What the law allows

The rules below apply to pooled tips in the US and the UK. They are the two markets most searches come from, and they differ in ways that catch people out.

United States

  • Who may participate. A pool may only include employees who customarily and regularly receive tips. Managers and supervisors may not take a share, even a token one.
  • Tip credit states. Where the employer takes a tip credit against the minimum wage, the employer may not keep any portion of tips. In states that abolished the tip credit, all tips belong to the employees regardless.
  • Service charges. An automatic 18% service charge on a large party is not a tip under federal rules — it is employer revenue, and how it is distributed is a matter of policy, not tip law. Some states treat it differently, so label it honestly on the menu.
  • Records. Keep the pool formula, the hours, and the distribution. A written policy is the difference between a clerical dispute and a wage claim.

United Kingdom

The Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023, in force since October 2024, requires employers to pass on 100% of tips, service charges and cover charges to workers, to allocate them fairly, and to keep a written policy. Agency workers are covered too. A statutory code of practice sets out what "fair" means and gives workers the right to request records and to bring a tribunal claim.

Canada, Ireland, and Australia

Ontario and Quebec have specific rules on tip pooling and employer deduction; British Columbia and Alberta rely more on employment standards and contract. Ireland's Payment of Wages Act and the 2022 tips legislation require electronic tips to be distributed fairly. Australia treats tips as voluntary and generally outside the award system, but a registered agreement can formalise a pool.

A fair-pool checklist

  1. Write the formula down and post it where the rota is posted.
  2. Record actual hours, including side work and cleaning time.
  3. Never let a supervisor or owner draw from the pool.
  4. Distribute on the same schedule as payroll, not "when we get to it".
  5. Show each person their own number, not just the total.
  6. Keep the records for at least the statutory period — three years in most US jurisdictions.

Frequently asked questions

Should tips be split by hours or equally?

Hours worked is the most defensible default. Equal splits are simpler and can suit a small team with identical shifts, but they penalise whoever takes the long or the quiet shift. Weighted points are the compromise when roles contribute differently.

Can managers or owners take part in a tip pool?

No under US federal rules. Managers and supervisors are excluded from pools that include customarily tipped employees, and in non-tip-credit states the employer may not retain any part of employee tips.

Do I have to report pooled tips for tax?

Yes. Pooled tips are taxable to the person who receives them. In the US, report $20 or more in a month to your employer on Form 4070. In the UK, tips run through payroll attract income tax and National Insurance.

Are tips counted in overtime calculations?

Usually not, because tips the employee keeps are excluded from the regular rate. They are included where the employer claims a tip credit, and mandatory service charges distributed by the employer count as wages.

How do I split the pool without odd cents?

Divide exactly, round each share down, then allocate the remaining units to the largest shares. Rounding every share to the nearest unit first is what makes the total drift.

What is tip-out versus a tip pool?

Tip-out passes a percentage of one person's own tips to support staff. A pool collects everything first and redistributes by formula. Different calculations, often different legal treatment.
