The formula, and why 2,080 hours

Converting an hourly wage to a salary is a multiplication, not a negotiation:

annual salary = hourly rate × hours per week × weeks per year
standard year = 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours

Payroll systems and lenders use 2,080 hours because it is the simplest defensible baseline. It assumes you are paid for all 52 weeks, including public holidays and paid vacation. The moment you take unpaid time, the real number is lower, which is why the calculator asks for unpaid weeks separately.

At $25.00 an hour: $25.00 × 40 × 52 = $52,000. Divided by 12 that is $4,333.33 a month, or $2,000 per biweekly paycheque.

Pay frequency and why the monthly number looks wrong

A biweekly schedule produces 26 paycheques a year, not 24. Two months out of twelve contain three pay dates, so your monthly income is not annual ÷ 12 even though the annual total is exactly right. Semi-monthly pay — the 15th and the last day — gives 24 cheques and a genuinely fixed monthly amount. If you are budgeting around rent, use the semi-monthly figure or the smallest monthly amount, not the average.

What the annual figure leaves out

Annual gross salary is the top line. The number that matters for a decision is usually one of these:

  • Total compensation — gross salary plus employer pension contributions, health insurance premium share, and any guaranteed bonus.
  • Take-home pay — after federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security and Medicare (7.65% combined in the US), and any benefit deductions.
  • Effective hourly rate — total pay divided by hours actually worked, which is the only fair way to compare a 35-hour salaried role with a 50-hour one.

Salaried versus hourly: the trade you are making

An hourly role pays for every hour and usually owes overtime after 40. A salaried exempt role pays the same for 40 hours or 55, and typically comes with paid leave, a predictable schedule, and benefits that hourly roles sometimes lack. Converting both to an effective hourly rate exposes the trade honestly: a $65,000 salary on a real 45-hour week for 50 weeks is $28.89 an hour, while $26 an hour on a guaranteed 40-hour week is $54,080 a year.

Neither is automatically better. What usually decides it is whether the salaried hours are genuinely 45 or quietly 55, and whether the hourly role guarantees hours during slow months.

Part-time and seasonal conversions

For 25 hours a week, the annual figure at $18.00 is $18.00 × 25 × 52 = $23,400. For a school-term job working 38 weeks, use 38 in the weeks field — the calculator will not assume you are paid across the summer. Contract and freelance work should be modelled with the freelance rate calculator instead, because unbillable admin time changes the maths completely.

Frequently asked questions

How many working hours are in a year?

2,080 for a standard 40-hour, 52-week year. Two unpaid weeks bring it to 2,000. Twenty-five hours a week is 1,300 hours.

Is $25 an hour a good salary?

It is $52,000 a year on a full-time schedule, about $4,333 gross a month. Whether that is comfortable depends on local rent, transport, and whether health insurance comes out of your pocket.

Does the annual figure include overtime?

The main result does not. Overtime is not guaranteed, so annualising it overstates recurring income. The optional overtime field shows the addition separately so you can see both numbers.

Should I use 52 weeks or 50?

52 if you are paid for every week. Fewer if you take unpaid leave, work seasonally, or bill clients only during term time.

How do I compare an hourly job with a salaried one?

Convert both to annual compensation, divide by realistic annual hours, then compare the effective rate. Add the value of paid leave, benefits, and schedule predictability before deciding.

Why is my monthly pay not annual divided by 12?

With biweekly pay you receive 26 cheques, so two months contain three pay dates. The annual total is still correct; only the monthly cash flow varies.
