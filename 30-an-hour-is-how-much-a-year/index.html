$30 an Hour Is How Much a Year?

Forty hours a week at $30.00 an hour is $62,400 a year before tax — about $5,200 a month, or $2,400 per biweekly paycheque. The tables below break it down by pay period and by how many hours you actually work.

How the figure is calculated

annual = rate × hours per week × weeks per year
$30.00 × 40 × 52 = $62,400

The 40 × 52 baseline is 2,080 hours, which is what payroll systems and lenders use. It assumes you are paid for every week of the year, including public holidays and paid vacation. Any unpaid week off lowers it: two weeks unpaid at $30 an hour is $2,400 less.

What comes out before it reaches you

On $62,400 of gross wages, the deductions that apply to almost every US employee are Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45% — 7.65% combined, or $4,774 a year at this rate. That part is not optional and does not vary by state.

Federal income tax is the variable one. It depends on your filing status, other household income, and the standard deduction for the year. A single filer earning $62,400 typically lands in the lowest bracket after the standard deduction, and may owe little or nothing. State income tax ranges from zero in nine states to over 9% in the highest-tax states. Benefits deductions — health insurance, retirement contributions — come out on top of that.

A realistic planning range for take-home pay at this rate is $48,672 to $54,912 before state tax. Use your own payslip for the real number; the range is for budgeting, not for filing.

Overtime changes the picture more than the rate does

At $30.00 an hour, overtime is paid at $$45.00. Five extra hours a week — a 45-hour week — takes the annual figure from $62,400 to $74,100, an increase of $11,700. That is worth more than a $2 an hour raise at 40 hours.

Note that not every salaried role is exempt from overtime, and not every hourly role gets it. Federal law requires 1.5× for hours over 40 in a workweek for non-exempt employees; some states add a daily threshold. Run your actual hours through the overtime calculator if the number matters for a decision.

What $30 an hour means in practice

Thirty dollars an hour is roughly where median full-time hourly earnings land in the United States. It is also a common threshold in job postings, which makes it a useful benchmark: below it you are comparing to the middle, above it you are above.

Above average for hourly work and enough for a single earner almost anywhere. In the most expensive metros it still means careful budgeting, not comfort.

Frequently asked questions

$30 an hour is how much a year?

$62,400 before tax at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. That is $5,200 a month gross, $2,400 per biweekly paycheque, and $1,200 a week.

$30 an hour is how much a year after tax?

Social Security and Medicare take $4,774 of that. Federal income tax depends on filing status and the standard deduction; state tax depends on where you live. Expect roughly $48,672 to $54,912 in take-home pay before state tax.

How much is $30 an hour biweekly?

$2,400 gross for 80 hours. With five hours of overtime in the period, add $$225.00.

What if I work 50 hours a week at $30 an hour?

The first 40 hours pay $1,200; the 10 overtime hours pay $$45.00 each, or $450. That is $1,650 a week and $85,800 a year, assuming the overtime is paid as federal law requires.

Is $30 an hour good pay?

Above average for hourly work and enough for a single earner almost anywhere. In the most expensive metros it still means careful budgeting, not comfort.

How do I compare $30 an hour to a salary offer?

Convert the salary to an effective hourly rate by dividing it by the hours you will really work, not by 2,080. A $62k salary on a genuine 45-hour week is $27.73 an hour over 50 working weeks — compare that against $30.00 with guaranteed hours.
