40 Hours a Week Is How Many Hours a Year?

The short answer is 2,080 — forty hours across fifty-two weeks. The useful answer depends on how many weeks you are actually paid for, which is where most conversions quietly go wrong.

Last updated 18 November 2026

Where 2,080 comes from

40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours

That is the number payroll systems, mortgage lenders, and salary-to-hourly converters use. It assumes you are paid for all 52 weeks of the year including public holidays and paid vacation.

It is a convention, not a description of your life. Anyone who takes unpaid leave, works seasonally, or contracts without billing during holidays works fewer hours, and dividing a salary by 2,080 in those cases produces a rate that is too low.

The variants that actually apply

ScheduleHours per yearTypical situation
40 hrs × 52 weeks2,080Standard payroll baseline
40 hrs × 50 weeks2,000Two weeks unpaid, or 10 days PTO taken
40 hrs × 48 weeks1,920Four weeks off, common in Europe
37.5 hrs × 48 weeks1,800UK full-time with 25 days leave
35 hrs × 52 weeks1,820Short full-time week
25 hrs × 52 weeks1,300Part-time
45 hrs × 50 weeks2,250Salaried with regular unpaid overtime

The 2,000-hour figure is a useful middle assumption for anyone who takes two unpaid weeks. It changes a $60,000 salary from $28.85 an hour to $30.00 — a 4% difference that matters when comparing offers.

Paid leave is not fewer hours worked

This distinction trips people up constantly. If you are paid for 52 weeks and take 15 days of paid holiday, you are paid for 2,080 hours. The hours you actually work are lower.

Both numbers are legitimate, for different questions:

  • Converting a salary to an hourly rate for comparison: use hours actually worked, because you are asking what your time is worth.
  • Checking a payslip: use paid hours, because that is what the payroll system divides by.
  • Calculating overtime: use hours worked in the workweek, which is a separate measure entirely.

A salaried employee on $60,000 with 15 days of paid leave works roughly 1,960 hours while being paid for 2,080. Their effective rate per hour worked is $30.61, not $28.85.

Overtime makes the annual hours number irrelevant

None of the figures above include overtime. A 45-hour week for 48 weeks is 2,160 hours worked, and the pay calculation splits it differently: 40 hours at straight time plus 5 at 1.5× for each of those weeks.

annual pay = (40 × rate × weeks) + (5 × rate × 1.5 × weeks)

At $20 an hour over 48 weeks that is $38,400 + $7,200 = $45,600. Dividing that by 2,160 hours gives $21.11 — the effective hourly rate, which is higher than the nominal $20.00 because five hours a week earn a premium.

This is why "hours per year" is the wrong denominator for anyone with regular overtime. The right one is total hours actually worked, and the right numerator is total pay.

Why lenders still use 2,080

Mortgage applications and income verification use 2,080 because it is standardised across applicants and produces a comparable number regardless of an individual's leave arrangements. It is a classification convention, not an attempt to describe your hours.

The consequence is that hourly workers with steady overtime sometimes have their income understated for lending purposes, because lenders may discount overtime that is not contractually guaranteed. If you are applying for a mortgage on overtime income, keeping two years of payslips that show the pattern is what makes it countable.

The calculation in one line

annual hours = hours per week × weeks actually paid

Everything else — whether the weeks are 52, 50, or 48, whether leave is paid or unpaid, whether overtime is involved — is a question about which of those two inputs you should use. Get those right and the multiplier is trivial.

Frequently asked questions

Is 2,080 hours a year correct?

It is correct for someone paid for 40 hours across all 52 weeks. It is wrong for anyone with unpaid leave, seasonal work, or a part-time schedule. Divide by the weeks you are actually paid for.

How many hours a year is 40 hours a week for 50 weeks?

2,000 hours. That is the right figure if you take two weeks unpaid, and it raises the implied hourly rate of a fixed salary by about 4% compared with dividing by 2,080.

How many working hours are in a year excluding holidays?

In the US, roughly 2,080 minus 8 hours for each holiday not worked, if the holiday is unpaid. With 10 unpaid holidays that is 2,000 hours. With paid holidays you are still paid for 2,080.

Does 2,080 include paid time off?

Yes, if the leave is paid. Paid leave means you receive money for those hours but do not work them. For an effective hourly rate based on time actually spent working, subtract the leave hours.

What is the annual hours figure for part-time work?

Multiply weekly hours by paid weeks. Twenty-five hours a week for 52 weeks is 1,300 hours; the same schedule for 48 weeks is 1,200.
